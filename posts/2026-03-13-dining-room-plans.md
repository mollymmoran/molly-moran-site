I love our first home. It checks all the practical boxes — three bedrooms, two bathrooms, a finished basement, a garage, a flat backyard (which can feel like winning the Kentucky real estate lottery), and beautiful natural light. When touring the house for the first time we could instantly see its potential and knew it was the right house for us - we are so happy to call it ours.

![](/images/closing.jpeg)

It was built in the 1950s, but aside from the charming front door, there aren’t many obvious nods to mid-century design. Which honestly? I’m not mad about. It’s a blank slate. And blank slates are basically invitations.

My design style leans traditional with a few modern touches to keep things feeling fresh (and not like you accidentally wandered into an antique mall). I love symmetry, classic furniture silhouettes, and architectural details — moldings, trim, built-ins — the kinds of things that make a home feel layered and intentional.

And the dining room? First on the list.

![](/images/diningroom_original.jpeg)

### **Why Start With the Dining Room?**

I spend a lot of time in this room. It’s where we eat every meal. It doubles as my laptop station. It becomes craft central when inspiration strikes (often!) It’s multifunctional in the most real-life way. They didn’t call me *Hobby Molly* in high school for nothing.

![](/images/cards.jpeg)

![](/images/crafts_example.jpeg)

If I’m going to live in a room this much, it might as well feel special.

I like to approach DIY renovations room by room. Breaking it down into manageable projects keeps it from feeling overwhelming (and keeps our budget from spontaneously combusting). Small, strategic upgrades over time add up.

##### **The Project List**

Here’s what’s on deck:

* Remove carpet, tack strips, and approximately 4,000 hidden staples
* Replace electrical outlets and light switches
* Trim out the cased openings
* Add built-in shelves/cabinets flanking the large window (craft storage yay!)
* Replace baseboards and add shoe molding
* Add crown molding
* Wallpaper?!
* Replace the chandelier
* Furnish & decorate

Nothing too wild. Just… everything!

### **Design Goals: Traditional, Modern, Functional**

Here’s the vision:

**1. Layered Architectural Charm**

I want to add decorative trim and moldings to create that collected, traditional feel. The house may not have been born with character, but we can absolutely give it some.

**2. Color & Pattern**

I want this room to feel like a hug. Cozy, welcoming, slightly dramatic in the best way. Think dinner parties that linger. Wallpaper that makes a statement.

**3. Smart Storage**\
\
Built-ins will (hopefully) solve two problems: storage and styling. I want a place to tuck away the not-so-pretty things and display the pretty ones — serving ware, collected pieces, maybe a few thrifted finds that look far more expensive than they were.

**4. Work With the Floors**

The orange-toned wood floors aren’t exactly what I would have chosen — but they’re beautiful. And they’re staying. So instead of fighting them, we’re embracing them. The right paint colors, wallpaper, and finishes can make them feel intentional.

Design is often less about perfection and more about perspective.

Thanks for being here — this is just the beginning.
