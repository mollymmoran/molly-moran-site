Over the past several months the dining room has come a long way, with more still to do! I’m celebrating progress over perfection here. Home design isn't something that happens overnight, and I'm learning to embrace that it evolves slowly - even unfinished and imperfect, this space still deserves to be enjoyed along the way. It’s taken lots of hard work, countless trips to the hardware store, and at least one minor meltdown over trim angles — but here’s where we stand.

![](/images/diningroom_header.jpg)

## Carpet Removal

My husband, Ethan, did most of the manual labor removing the carpet and padding before we moved in, and my brother, Josh, was kind enough to haul it away for us. We pulled a mind-boggling number of staples out of that floor, and every once in a while I still find one we missed — the gift that keeps on giving. A staple puller (previously used for upholstery projects) was the MVP for prying them up without damaging the floors, and a small crowbar handled the tack strips. The wood floors were in good shape underneath, much to our relief.

![](/images/dining-room-1.jpeg)

## Wallpaper Removal & Painting

We only had a chair-height border of wallpaper, so this was a quick and easy task, thanks to a spray bottle of Roman Liquid Wallpaper Remover and some plastic putty knives. For paint, we went with Sherwin Williams White Flour for the trim and bookshelves, and Dover White for the walls — a shade creamier, so things don’t feel too stark.

## Baseboard, Cased Opening Trim & Crown Molding

We had a lot of fun with this one, even though the miter cuts took some getting used to (read: several “wait, why doesn’t this fit” moments). Our original baseboards were simple and only 3.5” tall, so we upgraded to a 5.5” decorative baseboard. For the shoe molding, instead of typical quarter round, I used decorative door jamb pieces at 1.5” tall — a little idea I’m still patting myself on the back for, since it added another layer of detail. As first-time DIYers, we used the Kreg Crown-Pro jig for our crown molding cuts, and it was an absolute lifesaver — highly recommend if you don’t want to cry over a miter saw. We also added trim to the cased openings (one to the kitchen, one larger one to the living room) to keep the whole room feeling cohesive. Trim ended up being one of the most impactful, bang-for-your-buck ways to add character.

## Built-In Bookshelves

This was SO much work. Thankfully I had a vision and planned and researched extensively beforehand, because winging this one would not have gone well. I wanted each unit to have four shelves and two cabinet doors on the bottom for craft storage, and they’ve added exactly the character I was hoping for. They deserve a dedicated blog post to share how we pulled it off, look for that coming soon! For an entire month afterwards, we ate dinner just staring at them, half proud of ourselves and half in disbelief we actually pulled it off.

![](/images/dining-room-4.jpeg)

## Blinds, Curtains & Rug

We went with classic Roman shades from Select Blinds in white linen — I love that when they’re pulled up, you can still see the pretty window grille on the big window. We added simple navy linen curtains for some color and depth. They’re light-filtering and pinch-pleated, which gives a more tailored, structured look — leaning more traditional, which is just my style. Brass curtain rods were an easy pick too: warmer than bright gold, but not as cold as silver. I'm still debating wallpaper, so consider the current curtains and rug purely temporary - pending this very intimidating decision.
