Our bathroom was built in 1957, and it shows — in the best and most stubborn way possible. The peachy-pink color, small mosaic-style flooring, and half height tiled walls wrapping the room aren’t a design choice I made; they’re a design choice that was made for me, by postwar America’s unshakeable confidence in pastel ceramics. This isn’t the glossy, teak-and-geometric Mid-Century Modern you’d pin on a mood board — it’s something more honest than that: a practical, middle-class suburban bathroom built to last, which it has, infuriatingly and impressively, for nearly seven decades. It has held up well but is not my style, which is why I’ve spent an embarrassing amount of time lately creating mood boards wondering what comes next.


Small footprint, big potential — or at least that’s what I keep telling myself. I’m realistically a few years out from actually remodeling it, but that hasn’t stopped me from falling deep into the inspiration rabbit hole and creating three very different mood boards. And while each one takes the bathroom in a distinct direction, a few through-lines show up in all of them: basketweave floor tile, something interesting on the walls — whether that’s vertical V-groove paneling, wallpaper, or bead board — beautiful lighting, plus timeless fixtures. These are the things I keep gravitating back to no matter how many browser tabs I open, which feels like a good sign. If this bathroom is going to last another sixty-eight years, I’d rather bet on timeless than trendy.



![](/images/bathroom-option-1.jpeg)

## Mood Board 1

This one is quiet, simple, and just a little bit moody. A dark olive green vanity anchors the room — it’s the kind of color that reads as neutral until it absolutely doesn’t, in the best way. Creamy white v-groove paneling (in Sherwin Williams White Dove?? If you know - you know!) climbs the walls, greige subway tile in the shower, and a classic black-framed mirror ties it together without trying too hard. It’s calm, it’s classic, and it would age beautifully.




![](/images/bathroom-option-2.jpeg)

## Mood Board 2

This board commits to drama and earns it. A dark charcoal marble basketweave floor tile sets a serious tone from the ground up, balanced by white bead board paneling and a rich walnut-toned vanity with a reeded drawer front. The showstopper is the hexagonal shower tile: a bold, veined marble in deep burgundy, cream, and grey that brings serious character. I’ve had this tile pinned on Pinterest for years, and am still too scared to look at the price. An arched black-framed mirror echoes the formality without feeling stiff. Chrome fixtures keep the metallic palette cohesive.




![](/images/bathroom-option-3.jpeg)

## Mood Board 3

If this bathroom had a personality, it would bring you flowers — literally. The block-print floral stripe wallpaper is the star here, the kind of pattern that makes a small room feel like a destination rather than an afterthought. It’s the one element that may be more trendy than timeless, but I like it. A warm natural oak vanity keeps things grounded and fresh, while a wavy blue-framed mirror adds just the right amount of whimsy without tipping into precious. The blue-grey marble basketweave floor softens the contrast between the playful wallpaper and the square zellige shower tiles. This one has the most personality of the three — it’s cheerful, layered, and genuinely fun. I would love to get ready for the day in here.




Three mood boards, one bathroom, and zero decisions made — welcome to the remodel planning phase, where the ideas are endless and the budget is hypothetical. I’ll be living with all three of these for a while before I commit and make a realistic plan, but in the meantime I want to hear from you. Which direction would you take it?
